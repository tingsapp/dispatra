# Dispatra web client specification

Updated: 2026-10-14. This is the current React/TypeScript client contract. [Implementation state](state.md) distinguishes browser demo behavior from API-backed behavior.

## 1. Scope and authority

The web client serves dispatchers, company administrators, shippers, and platform administrators. The FastAPI/PostgreSQL API owns identity, tenant scope, pricing, assignment, route feasibility, POD and persistent operational records. The authenticated dispatcher route uses the manual API within the original Monitor, Pricing and form designs; `/prototype` is a separate local demonstration. Do not treat a local calculation, fixture or simulated GPS point as an authoritative server result.

Use React, strict TypeScript, Vite, shadcn/ui, Inter, a light neutral theme, black primary actions, blue accent, and shared accessible controls. Keep the existing Dispatra brand and compact visual style. The public homepage describes email intake, automatic dispatch and driver delivery, with a company identifier input below the hero description, a fixed `dispatra.com/` prefix and Start trial on its own row. Start trial opens `/signup?company={slug}` to review the plan and trial; reject reserved identifiers and full URLs. Sign in stays available beside the trial help and in the footer. Sign in opens the shared `/login` page without asking for a company. The shared public header places the Dispatra logo at the left and a black Login link styled as a button at the top right, opening `/login`. The centered single-column login keeps Dispatcher/Shipper/Driver choices above Email and Password, with Back to home beneath the form. Role selection uses a compact light gray pill track with three equal rounded segments, a white selected pill with a subtle shadow that slides between choices, dark selected text and muted inactive labels, adapting the supplied ChatGPT Chat/Work switch reference to the light theme. Keep the group legend accessible but visually hidden. Preserve native radio keyboard controls, an inset focus ring, disabled behavior and reduced-motion support. Switching roles retains the entered email, submits the matching API portal and clears stale errors. Successful authentication redirects to the returned account's company and role. Email and nonblank phone are unique within each role across all companies; different roles can share them. Company links remain valid entry points but never constrain authentication to the URL's company. `/shipper`, `/driver` and `/dispatch` initialize the matching role on the shared login. Session-derived resume uses the server account; remembered company preferences are ignored. Platform administration remains a separate owner login at `/admin`, without company-role choices. Successful owner login immediately opens `/admin/companies` from `/admin`, `/admin/login` and `/platform/login`; preserve valid admin deep links. Use the authenticated account response without reloading the login URL, cancel stale account reads and remove previous workspace queries. Prototype credentials remain isolated at `/prototype/login`. Never persist passwords. Public entry and login use shared accessible 40px controls and work on mobile and with a keyboard. Full-page startup loading messages share one centered status component.

The `/signup` trial page uses the shared public header and shows the saved monthly plan alongside a one-month free trial card (30 days). Read the public plan terms from `/api/v1/subscription/plan`; display the configured Decimal price and currency, or Contact us for pricing when unset. Show loading/error/retry states. Start free trial opens the signup form in a shared responsive popup with a scrollable body and fixed submit footer. Place the content-width Start trial action at the bottom right on desktop and mobile. Keep the underlying page inert and prevent background scrolling; provide focus trapping, Escape/backdrop/X dismissal and focus restoration. Dismissal clears transient credentials, and pending signup disables dismissal as well as fields/submission. Use Workspace as the shared field label, including its accessible homepage label. Preserve the company identifier from the homepage. Full name and Company name share the first row; Workspace and Address share the second row, and Email and optional Phone share the third row. Stack paired fields on mobile. Address reuses the shared Canadian Google Places autocomplete with manual-entry fallback, no coordinate lookup, a 500-character limit and pending lock. Save the chosen or manually entered text to company settings in the signup transaction; it remains optional for backwards compatibility. Wait for selected-place resolution before submitting. Escape closes open address suggestions before dismissing the popup. Place Password and matching Confirm password in a separate Account security section with a soft gray rounded background, no divider, shared length hint and two aligned columns on desktop that stack on mobile. Require an initially unchecked Terms and Conditions checkbox before signup; link Terms in a new tab so form drafts remain intact. The popup has no Privacy Policy text or link; Privacy stays in footer navigation. Lock it during pending signup and reset acceptance on dismissal. Acceptance is a frontend gate; the API does not currently persist a legal-consent record. The account is always the new company's dispatcher. Start trial creates the company/account/defaults through the typed API, queues an emailed six-digit code and opens `/signup/verify`; there is no credit-card field or forced password change. Require verification before workspace access, start the 30-day trial after a valid code, and explain that continued access requires Dispatra activation after the trial ends. Lock pending controls, keep the same operation key for an uncertain retry, preserve inputs on errors and clear passwords on success. Never persist signup credentials or grant a role/company selected in the browser. Company Profile and owner Overview display the saved trial end; expired companies use the existing Suspended/Activate interface. Existing company-free login remains separate from trial creation.

Platform administration has Subscription at `/admin/billing`, with only the Monthly subscription and One month free trial cards. Both cards have equal widths and heights, side by side on large screens and stacked on smaller screens; their shared height expands with content rather than clipping it. The menu and page title are Subscription; do not show or fetch the company subscription table on this page. Default price/currency are copied into each new company agreement; changes never overwrite existing agreements. Monthly prices are rates per registered vehicle per month; use this unit in the default plan, public signup and Revenue table. Every company has a Subscription tab, also available at `/admin/companies/{id}/subscription`, with owner-managed Not subscribed, Active, Paused or Cancelled status, a Price per vehicle per month field and start date. Render this as an unframed section, without the white card, descriptive helper copy, Currency field or Trial & workspace access section. Preserve the saved agreement currency automatically in updates. Active requires a price (zero is valid) and start date. Persist through generated owner APIs with expected version, payload-bound retry keys, explicit conflict reload and audit history. Preserve form drafts through background refresh and tab switches. Workspace activation/suspension and saved trial dates remain separate; subscription edits do not authorize workspace access or establish payment receipt.

Revenue at `/admin/revenue` has a shared navigation entry labelled Revenue and the same page title. Keep the page clean, with only the short description “Expected recurring revenue.” beneath its title. Remove the enclosing Recurring revenue card, dated introduction, long payment/access explanation and empty recurring-revenue message. When active agreements exist, show compact per-currency monthly recurring revenue and annualized projection (monthly × 12) metrics directly, without an explanatory wrapper. Each contributing company's monthly amount is its saved vehicle rate multiplied by its current non-archived registered vehicle count, including temporarily inactive vehicles. Zero vehicles contribute zero. Keep subscription counts and the searchable/paginated company table on Revenue without a Company subscriptions heading or extra description. Totals cover all saved records independently of table pagination/search. Only explicitly Active agreements whose UTC start date has arrived contribute; future Active agreements are Scheduled. Unconverted trials and Not subscribed, Paused and Cancelled agreements generate no revenue. The page links directly to each company's Subscription tab and retains loading/error/retry states and the company table's empty state. Amounts remain expected agreement revenue; no payments, collection or subscription invoices are created. Workspace access remains independent of the agreement.

The Revenue summary cards are ordered Total, Companies, Active subscriptions, Not subscribed, Paused and Cancelled. Remove the Scheduled summary card; scheduled agreements remain excluded from revenue and retain their table status. Total displays the API's exact all-company monthly revenue totals, separately per currency without conversion or adding unlike currencies. Display 0.00 when no agreements contribute. Preserve the existing per-currency monthly/annualized details, summary layout, loading/error handling and table filters; Total never depends on table search or pagination.

Every password entry field has an eye button to show/hide its value, including company/owner login, account security, admin initial/change passwords, prototype forms and mailbox app passwords. Passwords start hidden; each field controls its own visibility and becomes hidden again when cleared. The button has an accessible Show/Hide label, keyboard support and pressed state; it cannot submit the form or bypass disabled fields. Toggling preserves the exact entered value, validation and password-manager autocomplete. Visibility lives only in the mounted field.

The homepage map preview loops through sample email intake, order preparation, Auto assignment, pickup, delivery proof and completion logs. Show the latest three events with a synchronized order status and a pause/play control. Logs stay opaque and slide continuously through a clipped viewport; retain previous rows through the loop boundary rather than clearing or fading the feed. Reduced-motion preferences show a static completed view, and hidden tabs pause progression. The route follows the sample street network, with turns at intersections; pickup, delivery and driver markers share the SVG coordinate system so resizing never detaches them from the road. The preview stays explicitly illustrative and never reads or mutates operational records.

The homepage preview's Completed status uses a green label and checkmark.

The homepage and plans page share a spacious light footer: a trial call-to-action and 30-day/no-card note, brand summary, Explore links (Home, How it works, Plans & free trial), team portal links (Dispatchers, Shippers, Drivers), and a copyright row. Every link uses an existing public destination. Footer legal navigation links to `/terms` and `/privacy`. The homepage keeps the authenticated company/role Open my workspace link, falling back to Sign in to your workspace. The brand and call-to-action stack on mobile while the two compact link groups remain side by side; footer controls remain inside the inert background while the signup popup is open.

The public `/terms` and `/privacy` pages reuse the shared header/footer and readable responsive article layout, with policy cross-links and a return to plans. They are clearly labelled drafts for review. Copy reflects current account/trial/operational behavior without claiming compliance or inventing legal identity, contact details, provider locations or retention periods. The owner must review and finalize these details before public release. Privacy coverage follows the categories in [ICO privacy-notice guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/the-right-to-be-informed/what-privacy-information-should-we-provide/); this is drafting guidance, not a claim that UK law applies. Both route names are already reserved in web/API company identifiers.

## 2. Routes and access

Each checkout/preview keeps Vite optimized dependencies in its own `.local/vite-cache` directory, even when node_modules is shared by symlink. Concurrent previews must not replace the main app's optimized modules while it runs.

Both the owner Add company popup and public signup popup include an initially checked **Use Dispatra TMS** checkbox, with “Uncheck to connect an External TMS.” Checked means Dispatra TMS, the full workspace and normal company pricing. Unchecked means External TMS: save `tms_workspace=true` through the typed creation/settings APIs, open `/{slug}/integrations` after login, and show Profile, Integration, Help at the top with direct Log out instead of the account card. Monitor remains available in both modes through the Dispatra brand button and direct company-root URL. Auto dispatch retains its existing switch and saved mode in both workspaces. Other company creation and legacy records default to Dispatra TMS (`tms_workspace=false`); Shipper, Driver and owner navigation remain independent. The first Dispatra card in Connectors uses the same inverse checkbox meaning, explains both choices, and saves the preference through versioned company settings. In Dispatra mode show only this card; in External mode also show the six vendor connectors. The server switches the company Default to the existing imported-price card for External TMS, preserving its name and TMS-supplied total/tax; returning to Dispatra restores the previously selected active calculated Rate Card. Both changes commit together, retain other settings and existing order snapshots, and refresh rates/navigation immediately. Save failures retain the stored selection. The preference does not prove a connected TMS or change backend authorization.

| Path | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `/` | Public website and workspace entry |
| `/login`, `/dispatch`, `/shipper`, `/driver` | Shared login; aliases initialize the corresponding role |
| `/signup?company={slug}` | Public monthly plan and free trial, with signup popup |
| `/admin`, `/admin/companies`, `/admin/companies/{id}`, `/admin/profile`, `/admin/billing`, `/admin/revenue`, `/admin/companies/{id}/subscription` | Platform owner administration: companies, company detail with Overview, Subscription and History tabs, owner profile, Subscription, Revenue |
| `/{company-slug}/` | Dispatcher Monitor |
| `/{company-slug}/orders`, `/drivers`, `/vehicles`, `/shippers`, `/analytics`, `/pricing`, `/profile`, `/help` | Dispatcher pages |
| `/{company-slug}/integrations` | Dispatcher Integrations interface preview |
| `/{company-slug}/shipper`, `/shipper/profile` | Shipper portal (home is Orders) |
| `/{company-slug}/driver`, `/driver/profile` | Driver portal (home is Orders) |
| `/prototype` | Separate local demo entry |

Plural `/{company-slug}/shippers` and `/drivers` are dispatcher directories; singular `/shipper` and `/driver` are only the role portals, and no dispatcher page may use them. Dispatcher pages require the matching dispatcher API session; role-specific portals must not read dispatcher browser stores. A signed-in account that opens another role's area of its own company is sent to its own home. Older `/{slug}/shipper-portal/...`, `/{slug}/customer` and `/{slug}/dispatch` links redirect to the clean paths, and `/platform` still opens administration. Company slugs cannot use reserved top-level words (`RESERVED_SLUGS` in `src/lib/pageRoutes.ts`, mirrored by the API). Internal navigation preserves the slug, Back/Forward, direct loading, drafts, and unsaved-change confirmation.

## 3. Shared navigation and Monitor

The Needs Attention warning icon is orange; its metric background, count and label retain their neutral styling. The top-right group contains Dispatra AI and Notifications. Dispatra AI replaces the separate Monitor search field with company-scoped questions and lookup for Orders, stop addresses, drivers, vehicles and shippers. Matching record controls open the existing authorized Order or directory details. Other page search fields remain.

Use one sidebar/account menu with direct Profile and Settings access. The supplied Dispatra logo contains its own wordmark: public headers/footers, prototype login and expanded sidebars display it at its natural proportions without duplicate text. Compact sidebars and homepage map previews crop the icon from the same asset; accessible home/Monitor/expand labels remain on the controls. A compact icon rail is available when collapsed; mobile navigation overlays the page with keyboard focus management. The `Auto dispatch` switch exposes exactly `AUTO` and `MANUAL` and, in company workspaces, saves the company `dispatch_mode` (turning AUTO on asks for confirmation). MANUAL waits for dispatcher assignment and then optimizes; AUTO lets the API's Dispatch agent assign priced Orders that are due soon. For an unassigned Order, the Monitor's AI Recommendation shows the agent's ranked feasible drivers with reasons, labels a rules fallback, and lists why other drivers cannot take it; Approve assigns through the API, which re-checks every rule. AI can recommend but cannot bypass hard constraints. Local simulation is not proof of backend assignment.

Monitor opens directly on the Vancouver Google map without an Earth intro or pre-animation. Use Google Maps JavaScript API through `@vis.gl/react-google-maps`, standard roadmap style, optional satellite/traffic/labels, custom nearby marker cards, and route lines from saved planned stops. Keep map controls and non-map alternatives usable. Preserve the map instance during internal page navigation and pause hidden polling. A missing key or failed map load gives a clear inline message. Monitor list popups use the shared 18px medium dialog title, 13px row labels/metadata, compact status badges and neutral cards, with scrollable bodies inside rounded surfaces. The driver list popup is titled Drivers; its menu footer says View more and opens the same list. Driver cards show name, plain public ID, vehicle and only supplied next-stop/ETA values; searches/filters have explicit empty states, and mouse/keyboard selection uses the internal driver ID. Shared dialog focus trapping, Escape, × and backdrop dismissal apply. Monitor menu headings use medium weight; order/driver labels are 13px, and shared status/count badges use 12px medium text, a 16px line height, subtle fills and no heavy border. Keep status labels on one line, render driver public IDs as plain muted metadata, and stack name/status on phones. Floating panels clip their rounded outer shell while scrolling in an inset inner viewport with a thin scrollbar; scrollbar tracks never replace the rounded right corners.

Monitor pickup and drop-off stops reuse the homepage's circle glyph: white with a dark outline for pickups, dark with a white outline for drop-offs. Each located stop selects its Order using mouse, Enter or Space and names its type and address accessibly. When the Order status location coincides with a stop, place the status tag above that circle rather than covering it with a separate icon. Orders without a matching located stop retain their status marker. Every active order in the Active Orders menu and full list has an independent Show on map checkbox, checked by default. Unchecking removes all of that order's stop/status markers and road route without closing the menu or changing operational data. The menu continues listing hidden orders; its searches/filters do not change map visibility. Keep choices by internal order ID for the mounted workspace, across refreshes and internal navigation; new active orders default to visible, while Completed/Cancelled orders are excluded. The initial fit and Overview use only visible order points plus driver locations. Hiding the selected order closes its map popover; selecting a hidden order opens its full details without revealing its map layers. Driver markers stay visible.

Live updates and notifications: every authenticated workspace (dispatcher, Shipper portal, web Driver portal) runs one authenticated WebSocket feed (`src/portal/sync.ts`, `/api/v1/companies/{slug}/ws`) with the existing `/sync` HTTP loop as fallback. It resumes from the last applied cursor, monitors 15-second server heartbeats, reconnects with backoff, and recovers immediately when the tab becomes visible or the network returns. Disconnected polling waits the server-chosen `next_poll_ms`. Changes carry identifiers only and invalidate the affected TanStack Query reads; `reset` refetches everything. Pages do not add their own record polling. GPS events immediately invalidate Monitor and authorized Order tracking reads. The dispatcher Monitor also keeps its 30-second refresh for location freshness and time-based attention, and Shipper tracking retains its freshness refresh. The Monitor bell, and the header bell in the Shipper and Driver portals, show the account's API inbox: unread count from `/sync`, newest 50 notifications, critical/warning/info icons with text labels, read on open and Mark all read. Opening a dispatcher notification selects its Order on the map; Shipper notifications open Orders; driver notifications open Orders. Platform administration places the same bell/dropdown in the persistent top workspace header on every admin page, aligned with the right edge of the content below through shared width, responsive gutters and reserved scrollbar space. The button has a neutral background; Add company and All companies remain beside the page title. Its owner-only inbox refreshes every 15 seconds and after reads. It shows new company settings updates and dispatcher password changes, plus critical operational issues, disconnected order mailboxes and uncertain quote-email delivery across companies, with company labels, an unread indicator/count, newest 50 notices, read-on-open and Mark all read. A notice opens the relevant company Overview, never an operational Order. Empty/loading/error states and Escape/focus dismissal are shared with company portals. `/prototype` keeps its static sample alerts.

Order, Quote, Shipper warehouse, Driver, Company, and portal address fields use Canadian Google Places suggestions with delayed search, a selected-address session token, minimal requested fields, outside dismissal, keyboard support, and manual text entry. Selecting an address retains formatted text, city, province, country, postal code and coordinates; editing the text clears stale selection metadata. Order/Quote stops retain selected coordinates. A driver's required address supplies the driver's service city; current local assignment compares it to the Order pickup city; the API enforces this for persisted orders.

## 4. Dispatcher directories and forms

- **Shippers:** New/Edit uses Shipper name for both Business and Individual; Business also requires Company name. Collect email, phone, one complete Warehouse Address, rate card, optional discount, and instructions. Email is required for portal login and is also the quote and delivery-update address. There is no Contact name or Service Area input. The warehouse address is the default Order pickup and is preserved when the dispatcher manually changes that stop. V1 has no payment terms, invoicing or payment collection.
- **Drivers:** New/Edit requires name, phone, email, and address. Address city defines the service area; remove the old Service areas input. A DG qualification checkbox records whether the dispatcher has confirmed that the driver can handle dangerous goods; an Order with any DG package can be assigned only to a driver with this qualification. Every driver uses the company maximum active Orders limit; there is no individual override and no employment type. Drivers carry no billing details: no payout shares, earnings or pay records in this version. Attached vehicle selection begins with Register new vehicle and returns to the same driver draft. Editing Duty saves the dispatcher-selected On/Off state through the API and reloads from the persisted duty session; it does not start location sharing. With no image, show the uppercase first letter of the name as the avatar.
- **Driver details:** Two tabs. Details holds the profile and ends with Activity (app connectivity, app last seen, GPS captured, location permission and current route; no order count). Orders lists every Order assigned to this driver in any status, newest first, with number, status, shipper, route leg and completion or scheduled time.
- **Vehicles:** One fleet view and one Register/Edit form. The first list column is ID, showing the unit-based public ID with the saved year underneath (or a dash when unknown). Select a vehicle type, then edit vehicle-specific capacity and pricing. Cargo length, width, and height are required; Maximum stops is optional and a positive whole number. Equipment uses checkboxes. Keep one Vehicle description. Do not show Vehicle class name/description, Vehicle upgrade surcharge, Surcharge is fuel-eligible, or Requires commercial driver licence in the form; legacy stored values remain readable.

The Shippers list uses the shared table surface, typography, spacing, separators and hover treatment, neutral circular avatars, plain rate-card names and active-order counts, and shared icon actions. Shipper names open details with mouse or keyboard. Shipper details reuse the shared dialog, plain sections, read-only fields and order-summary cards. Use a neutral avatar and contact/notes text, with semantic colors reserved for status badges in both the list and details.

All create/edit/delete actions use shared dialogs and confirmations. List tables retain a visible Action column when they have actions. Open menus and dropdowns dismiss on outside click.

## 5. Orders, quotes, dates, and analytics

Every order-table address column is labeled Pickup → Delivery. All displayed times use 24-hour HH:mm (00–23) without AM/PM, including order lists/details, tracking, email intake, quotes, driver activity and platform administration. Preserve existing company/device time-zone choices and API ISO timestamps. Time pickers use 24-hour controls.

Orders list and Analytics use the same date menu: Today, Tomorrow, All dates, Date range. Analytics presents four summary cards in order (Total, In progress, Completed, Canceled) using the selected date range. In progress counts only IN_PROGRESS orders, Canceled counts CANCELLED orders, and absent statuses show zero. Analytics has exactly three Recharts charts: a Delivery performance donut (4/12 desktop width), a multi-line Orders, drivers & shippers activity chart beside it (8/12), and a full-width source chart with black bars below. Charts stack on mobile. Keep the single page description directly below the Analytics title; chart cards contain titles, data, legends and tooltips without descriptions or explanatory captions. The donut groups completed orders into On time, Late and Not measured, with a completed total in its center and labeled counts. Analytics has no Export CSV action. Activity counts orders and distinct assigned driver/ordering shipper IDs per scheduled company-calendar period; unassigned orders do not count as drivers. Use smooth solid curves with rounded ends and joins, no normal or hover circle markers, and a labeled legend. A single date shows its counts as short solid line segments. Use scheduled company-calendar dates consistently. Include unmeasured completed deliveries separately; unknown timing is never a zero-percent result. Only accepted delivery evidence counts as verified POD. Revenue and accessorial-charge reporting are outside this page. The Orders date filter sits with list filters; New Quote and New Order remain header actions. New is in the status selector, so there is no separate Unassigned control. Analytics date selection updates every KPI and chart from dated records. Valid completion retains the booked price; Completed is the final successful Order status. V1 has no invoice actions, documents, emails or billing review holds.

New Order chooses a saved Shipper. New Quote is for a prospect: it chooses an active Rate Card instead of a Shipper, offers Send Quote, and never creates an Order. Send Quote creates a persisted Quote and explicitly queues email delivery through the API. A PDF or mail-client action is only a local export or handoff when offered. Neither form shows manual Zone, Priority, or Residential controls; Residential is an Accessorial. Each package has Fragile and DG checkboxes linked to editable Accessorials of the same names: checking a package selects that Accessorial, and the charge applies to each flagged unit in the package row's Qty. Checking or clearing the Accessorial in the list sets or clears that flag on all packages. Package inputs share a single flexible row, display one decimal place, stay at least 50px wide, and scroll within the Packages container only when necessary. Compact fields are about 32px high. Multiple pickup/drop-off links use explicit From/To selectors.

The dispatcher Order and Quote forms offer Add accessorial within the Accessorials list. It creates a company catalogue Accessorial using the same name, rate, description and taxable fields as Settings, then selects it for the current draft and refreshes the estimate. Shipper self-service can select configured Accessorials but cannot create catalogue entries.

Order and Quote form sections have plain titles without sequence numbers. Pickup and drop-off badges show their type without a sequence number; stop-specific controls and multi-stop links still identify individual stops. Pickup and drop-off date selectors show `Date` when empty. New bookings disable past dates and elapsed time slots in the company time zone. Each drop-off begins no earlier than its latest linked pickup; the same day is allowed when the delivery time follows the pickup time. Submission rechecks pickup and delivery times against the current clock and linked pickup times. Future hours and all minutes remain available because the company has no working-hours setting and after-hours service is supported. Editing an existing Order retains its saved schedule. Pickup and drop-off contact phone numbers are optional.

Order lifecycle is `NEW → ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, with `CANCELLED` separately. Pricing review, At risk, Late start, and Failed attempt are attention flags, not competing lifecycle states. An Order is the commercial pricing unit; Routes contain multiple Orders. The details view shows saved charge lines, subtotal, tax, and total without a View calculation disclosure. Frozen pricing is not silently changed by assignment or directory edits.

Order details omit Priority and dismiss through the top-right X, Escape or backdrop, without a redundant footer Close button in dispatcher or shipper popups. Completed orders have no Locate on Monitor action in the details popup or Orders list. Open orders retain their locate action. Dispatcher detail footers render only when Edit, Complete or Locate actions are available; shipper Track/Edit actions remain.

Every Order details popup identifies the saved creation source as Dispatcher created, Shipper created, Email or External TMS (`IMPORT`). API records retain their creation source independently of editable pricing input. An External TMS order also shows its saved `external_reference` as the TMS reference number, and indicates when a historical record has no reference. Missing or unknown historical sources show Not recorded; do not infer the source from the Rate Card or a general PO/reference field. The Integrations preview does not yet import TMS Orders.

## 6. Settings and company profile

Profile tabs are Company and Security. Company starts with one logo, Company name, Contact full name, email, phone, role, and a single-line address; Save Company sits at the bottom. On authenticated company routes, account email and role come from the session and are read-only. Company details, taxes and preferences load/save through the versioned company-settings API, and password changes use the authentication API. There is no personal avatar editor. Taxes and Preferences are separate Settings tabs, each with its own Save: Taxes contains an optional tax registration number, checkboxes labeled `Apply GST/HST to taxable charges` and `Apply provincial tax to taxable charges`, and rates; Preferences contains regional preferences (currency, time zone and units). Disabled taxes do not charge. Company phone and billing email are not in General settings.

The Settings page (formerly Pricing; `/{company}/settings`, with `/pricing` still accepted) has tabs Rate Cards, Service Level, Accessorials, Fuel Surcharge, Taxes, Vehicle Types, Preferences and, in company workspaces only, Mailbox: the pricing tabs follow how a price builds up (base rate, service, accessorials, fuel, tax), then fleet and company setup. Mailbox holds the Order agent's IMAP connection and lists emails the agent could not read; Profile contains only Company and Security. Vehicle Types lists company vehicle types with Name, Max payload, Pallet capacity and inside Cargo length, width and height in the company weight and dimension units; types can be added, edited and deleted (delete deactivates, so registered vehicles keep their details). The Register Vehicle and vehicle edit forms offer the active types and fill capacity and cargo size from the chosen type. A Rate Card is the base customer price; shipper discounts are set on the Shipper form. Service Level uses one fixed additional charge, and exactly one service is the company Default (badge plus a "Set as default" action; the Default cannot be deleted): new dispatcher and Shipper orders start on it unless the Shipper has its own default service, and the Order agent uses it when an email names no service. Mailbox has no service setting; Fuel Surcharge uses one editable percentage; Accessorials are configured separately. Service Level and Accessorial rows use a small confirmed delete icon in an Action column. Preserve historical saved terms and frozen quotes when retiring controls.

New Rate Cards support Base + Distance, Fixed, Zone to zone, and Hourly; existing Imported cards remain readable/editable. Base + Distance uses base fee plus distance beyond included distance, without a weight charge. Fixed uses its fixed amount. Zone uses one central pickup and destination-zone columns over From/To weight bands, with a 0–99 lb / Zone 1 / $20 starter rate, Add row, and a small delete icon per row. A missing zone/weight rate requires review. Hourly uses rounded billable time and its Minimum Billable time, which is its only minimum: Hourly cards have no dollar Minimum Charge. Every Distance, Fixed, Zone and Hourly card has Apply fuel surcharge and Apply vehicle surcharge checkboxes. New-card defaults: fuel is on for every method; the vehicle surcharge is on for Distance and Fixed and off for Zone (weight bands already price load size, and the suggested vehicle follows that weight) and Hourly (the hourly rate prices the truck). The company sets the fuel % and each vehicle type's Surcharge (Settings → Vehicle Types). Every order carries a Required vehicle type decided at booking, never by the assigned driver: dispatcher New Order and the Shipper booking suggest the smallest general type the declared load fits (weight, pallets, item size) until someone picks one, and refrigerated or flatbed types are chosen explicitly. Its surcharge is part of the quote. Assignment is meets-or-exceeds: any driver's truck that has the required body (Refrigeration, Open deck) and fits the load may take the order, and assignment never reprices it. The dispatcher live estimate lists Vehicle Surcharge and Fuel Surcharge whenever the card applies them, at $0.00 when nothing is charged. Each card's `How pricing works` section shows an example calculated by the pricing engine, with no prose formula: the values used (the rate-card amount is called the Delivery charge, never Freight), then short Subtotal, Tax and Total equations. When the card has a minimum charge, a Charges line adds up the charges and the Subtotal line compares them with the minimum, for example `Subtotal = $50.00 minimum < $137.77 = $137.77` or `Subtotal = $500.00 minimum > $54.20 = $500.00`; max() notation and prose explanations are not used.
